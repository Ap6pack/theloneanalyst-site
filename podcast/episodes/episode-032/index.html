ANALYST: Okay, this next one is the one I've been waiting all episode for, because it's the cleanest spy story we've got and it's hiding inside a technical explainer. Distillation.

SKEPTIC: Georgetown's CSET, yeah. And let me actually define it, because people throw the word around. There are two flavors. In white-box, you can see the model's internals. In black-box, you can't.

ANALYST: Black-box is the interesting one. You only get the answers.

SKEPTIC: Right. In black-box distillation, the student is only trained on the teacher's final generated outputs, whereas white-box distillation can use the teacher logits to capture its entire probability distribution. So the student model just asks the teacher a million questions and learns from the replies.

ANALYST: It's academic plagiarism with extra steps. You don't steal the professor's notes, you just enroll in the class, write down every answer, and then open a competing university.

SKEPTIC: That's... not a terrible analogy. And the policy fear is specifically about Chinese labs doing this to US frontier models. That's what the whole piece is written to inform.
